=== VALIDITY
No. These simulators do not suspend Ammy as a phone would, so 4 Nov's suspension was not reproduced and the no-hold control never went quiet the way a suspended phone app does.
- C-nohold kept a continuous probe and the same pid on both iOS 27.0 and iOS 26.5.
- RunningBoard said why, word for word: "Ignoring suspend because this process is simulated and has background audio capabilities".
- On top of that, the simulator's in-process audio HAL (HALRBSAssertionGlue) takes "MediaPlayback" and "AudioRecording" RunningBoard assertions on the first resume and never releases them. That happens even when engine.start is refused. With them held, backgroundTimeRemaining stays DBL_MAX and the hold can never expire (scenarios A-I).
- Only with setActive refused from launch, before the graph is touched (J/K/L), did the simulator count background time: about 29-30 s, as on a phone (bgleft 29.7 / 29.1 / 28.3 at the first background reading). Even then it only starved the process of CPU time; it didn't suspend it.

So in this lab the hold's ladder, saves, expiry handler, counters and journal are real. "The hold keeps a process alive that iOS would suspend" is not proven. The re-hold kill is RunningBoard's own task-assertion enforcement. The simulator makes it more frequent than a phone would, because the process keeps running after expiry instead of being suspended.

=== ISSUES
- The hold reopens after it expires, and RunningBoard kills the app. timeUp() ends the task but leaves running, the 10 s timer and verify() in place. The next verify(), often in the same second, calls holdOpen() again: hold is .invalid, so beginBackgroundTask runs with no background time left. UIKit had already fired its expiry handlers, so the new task's handler never runs and nothing ends it. RunningBoard then terminates the process: code 0x2182BAD2, 'Timed-out waiting for process [app<com.local.ammy>:22386] to invalidate assertion ... Shared Background Assertion 3'. Seen in K-hold and L-hold on both iOS 26.5 and iOS 27.0, 0.6-4 s after the reopen. Journal: 'hold expired after 51s ...' then, the same second, 'verify: engine stopped / hold opened (found stopped)'. A reopen 9 s after expiry sometimes got 25 s; the next one was killed. Results: (a) a long failure ends with Ammy dead rather than suspended, so the .ended wake the class comment counts on ('whatever runs the process next (.ended, the person, a Shortcut)') can no longer come; (b) hold_expiries never reaches the receiver; (c) on a phone this is the watchdog-kill class the code comments warn about. Suggested fix: after timeUp, don't open a hold in the background until the silence has played or the app has been active again (a flag set in timeUp, cleared in recovered() and on becoming active). Or refuse to begin one when applicationState is .background and backgroundTimeRemaining is under a few seconds.
- The expiry handler needs the main thread, and real audio calls can hold it past the deadline. iOS 27.0, J-hold: once the lab stopped injecting refusals, a retry went into the real AVAudioSession/AVAudioEngine path. The probe (main run loop) went silent from +2 s to +14 s and again after +17 s. The journal's last line is 'verify: engine stopped' with no outcome, and RunningBoard killed pid 9471 at +36 s (0x2182BAD2) for 'Shared Background Assertion 1', the only hold, while hold_expiries was still 0. Only seen in the simulator, whose audio HAL is in-process; whether setActive or engine start can block this long on a phone is unknown. A mitigation not tied to the main thread would be to end the task from a utility-queue deadline (endBackgroundTask is thread-safe) a few seconds before backgroundTimeRemaining runs out.
- Minor, from the counters: during a long outage verify() from the 10 s timer and from each push fires tries beside the ladder. That came to about one try every 2.9 s (69 refusals in 200 s; 130+ journal lines out of the 400 kept). Harmless in itself, but each try is another real setActive/engine call, which matters given the issue above.

=== UNVERIFIED
- Whether the hold keeps a phone running after 4 Nov's failure: the simulator never suspends an app with the audio background mode, so the no-hold control never reproduced 4 Nov.
- How much background time a phone grants after an interruption stops the engine. The simulator's ~30 s was measured only with no audio ever started in the process.
- Whether a phone also kills the app when the hold is reopened after expiry, and whether a .ended delivered to a suspended phone app would get fresh background time for a new hold. The mechanism is RunningBoard's on both, but the simulator kept the process running after expiry, where a phone would suspend it.
- Whether AVAudioSession.setActive or engine start can block the main thread on a phone long enough to miss the expiry deadline. Seen only in the simulator, whose audio HAL is in-process.
- Real interruptions (calls, Siri, alarms), real 'what' or '!pri' refusals, route changes and media-services resets: the lab used synthetic notifications and injected errors.
- A device crash report (0x8badf00d): the simulator logs RunningBoard's 0x2182BAD2 termination but writes no .ips file.
